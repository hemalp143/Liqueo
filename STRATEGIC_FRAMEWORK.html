Liqueo

Knowledge Discovery & Reuse for Financial Consultants

This strategic framework explains how Liqueo solves the knowledge discovery and reuse challenge for financial consultants. It answers 11 essential questions about users, workflows, AI assistance, and the complete cycle of knowledge contribution and discovery.

1
Who is the user?

Financial and management consultants in strategy, operations, and M&A who face recurring client challenges and need to leverage institutional knowledge efficiently.

Primary User Segments:

  • Project managers planning new engagements
  • Senior consultants seeking precedent for complex situations
  • Engagement leads identifying team expertise and past approaches
  • Partners evaluating proposals and resource allocation
  • Analysts finding comparable cases for benchmarking

User Context: Consultants typically have 5-15 years of experience, manage multiple engagements simultaneously, and need instant access to what the firm has learned. They face time pressure: proposals due in days, client presentations tomorrow, and no time to manually search through archives.

Pain Point: Valuable knowledge exists—in past project files, emails, team members' heads—but is impossible to find when you need it. A consultant with exactly the right experience is unavailable. The same problem gets solved repeatedly with different (or worse) solutions.

2
What problem are they trying to solve?

Knowledge fragmentation and reuse friction. Consultants face three interconnected problems:

1. Undiscoverability: Past engagements are filed by project name or client, not by the problems they solved. A consultant working on "retail bank digital transformation" must manually search for cases, then manually assess relevance.

2. Inefficient Synthesis: Even when similar cases are found, extracting what's relevant—the approach, the timeline, the lessons learned—requires reading full engagement files. Critical insights are buried in hundreds of pages.

3. Lost Learning: Each completed engagement represents institutional knowledge, but it's rarely extracted or structured for reuse. What worked, what didn't, and why remains locked in project closure reports.

The Cost of Knowledge Loss
Without Liqueo, a consultant faces:
• 3 hours manually searching archives
• 2 hours reading candidate documents
• 1 hour extracting relevant insights
• Duplicates the work a colleague did 6 months ago

With Liqueo: 15 minutes to search, analyze, and discover relevant patterns.

Business Impact: Slower proposal development, inconsistent approaches across similar engagements, and lost competitive advantage from not leveraging what the firm already knows.

  • Proposal Development: New RFP arrives; must quickly assess scope, approach, and timeline. "Have we done this before?"

  • Risk Mitigation: Project hits a challenge; searching for how similar challenges were overcome in past engagements.
  • Resource Allocation: Partner needs to staff a specialized role; looking for past engagements with similar requirements to identify expertise.

The Trigger Pattern: The user has a specific problem or question, has limited time, and knows the firm has likely solved something similar. They need to find, understand, and act on that knowledge within hours or days—not weeks.

4
How do they search for knowledge?

Liqueo uses semantic search powered by AI embeddings, which understands meaning beyond keywords:

Search Methods:

  • Semantic Query: "We need to optimize cloud infrastructure and reduce costs" finds engagements about technology consolidation, even if those exact words aren't used.
  • Industry Filter: Narrow by industry (Technology, Healthcare, Finance, Retail) to find domain-specific patterns.
  • Transaction Type Filter: Filter by engagement type (M&A, Restructuring, Digital Transformation) to find structurally similar cases.
  • Hybrid Matching: Combines semantic similarity with keyword-based fallback when embeddings aren't available (graceful degradation).

Why Semantic Search Matters: A consultant searching "technology cost reduction" would get zero results if searching keyword-only. Semantic search understands that "retail infrastructure consolidation" and "IT back-office reorganization" are relevant even with different terminology.

Search Behavior Example
Query: "Bank reorganization with cost efficiency"

Keyword-only would find:
• Documents with those exact words
• Misses structurally similar cases

Semantic search finds:
• "Investment Bank Back-Office Reorganization" (40% match)
• "Retail Chain Restructuring" (62% match)
• "Financial Services Process Consolidation" (78% match)
• All ranked by relevance, not just keyword overlap

Result Presentation: Top 5 results ranked by relevance score (as percentage), with industry, type, value, and duration visible at a glance.

5
How does AI help them find the most relevant information?

AI plays three roles in discovering and prioritizing relevant information:

1. Semantic Understanding (Embeddings): AI converts engagement text into semantic vectors, so searches understand meaning rather than just matching words. "Cost optimization" and "expense reduction" are understood as equivalent.

2. Recommendation Engine: Beyond search results, AI identifies patterns across similar engagements and recommends:

  • Suggested consulting approaches from comparable cases
  • Potential challenges to anticipate (identified patterns)
  • Estimated effort and timeline based on similar work
  • Industry-specific success factors

3. Synthesis & Insights: AI analyzes multiple similar engagements together and generates:

  • Strategic recommendations for the current situation
  • Aggregated lessons learned across similar cases
  • Industry trend analysis
  • Risk identification and mitigation strategies

The Key Difference: Without AI, a consultant gets a list of documents to read. With AI, they get a curated understanding of what works, why, and what to watch for.

How are relevant results presented?

Liqueo presents results with progressive disclosure: summary first, details on demand:

Search Results View:

  • Relevance Score: 0-100% indicating how closely the engagement matches your query
  • Title & Metadata: Engagement name, industry, transaction type at a glance
  • Key Outcomes: One-line summary of what was achieved
  • Quick Details: Value ($M), duration (months), client name
  • View Button: Click to open full modal with complete details

Detail Modal (on click): When a consultant clicks "View", a modal popup displays:

  • Complete engagement details and description
  • Consulting approach used
  • Key outcomes and impact metrics
  • Associated files and documents
  • Metadata and creation date

Recommendations View: When using the recommendation engine, each result shows:

  • Relevance percentage and reasoning why it's relevant
  • Suggested approach based on the similar case
  • Potential challenges to anticipate
  • Estimated effort

Synthesis View: AI-generated insights presented as:

  • Strategic recommendations summarizing multiple cases
  • Industry trend analysis
  • Common success factors and risk areas
  • Timeline and resource estimates

Design Principle: Information scales from scannable (relevance + headline) to detailed (full engagement modal) based on user interest. No cognitive overload; everything visible in 1-2 clicks.

7
How do they determine whether existing knowledge is reusable?

Consultants evaluate reusability through structured assessment in the workflow:

Relevance Assessment (Step 5 of Workflow): Consultants review multiple dimensions:

  • Domain Fit: How similar is the client industry/context? Is the pattern transferable?
  • Structural Similarity: Are the challenges structurally equivalent, even if the domain differs?
  • Timeline Alignment: Is the estimated duration reasonable for the current engagement?
  • Resource Comparison: Do we have similar expertise and team capacity?
  • Outcome Relevance: Did the past engagement achieve results applicable to this situation?

Adaptation Decision: Based on this assessment, the consultant decides:

  • Direct Reuse: Approach is directly applicable; minimal adaptation needed
  • Adapt & Customize: Core approach is sound; tailor specifics to this situation
  • Reference Only: Useful for context/benchmarking but not directly applicable
  • Not Applicable: Different domain/context; keep searching
Reusability Assessment Example
Current Challenge: "Retail bank digital transformation"
Found Case: "Investment bank back-office reorganization"

Assessment:
• Domain: Different (retail vs investment) → -20%
• Structure: Technology + process consolidation → +40%
• Timeline: Similar scale (10-12 months) → +15%
• Outcome: Cost reduction achieved → +25%

Decision: Adapt the core approach; customize for
retail banking specific challenges

Workflow Support: The structured 9-step workflow explicitly includes "Review & Evaluate" and "Select Content to Reuse" steps, forcing deliberate assessment rather than haphazard borrowing.

8
How does AI help them understand and synthesise the knowledge that has been found?

AI synthesizes knowledge through multiple LLM-powered analysis modes:

Synthesis Engine (Synthesizer Module): Takes multiple similar engagements and generates:

  • Strategic Recommendations: "Based on 3 similar healthcare transformations, here's what works..."
  • Aggregated Learnings: Extract common success factors, typical challenges, resource requirements
  • Industry Analysis: Trends across all engagements in a specific sector
  • Risk Synthesis: Consolidate risks identified in similar cases

Three-Mode Operation (Graceful Degradation):

  • Full Mode (LLM Available): Uses Claude API for sophisticated synthesis and strategic insight
  • Hybrid Mode (Partial Availability): Combines available LLM with keyword/metadata analysis
  • Manual Mode (No LLM): Generates insights from engagement metadata, structured patterns, and templates
Synthesis in Action
Input: 3 similar engagements on "cost optimization"

AI Synthesis Output:
"Across three comparable engagements:

Approach: All used 3-phase model (assess, design, implement)
Timeline: Average 11 months, range 9-14 months
Success Factors: Strong sponsorship (3/3), change mgmt
Challenges: Stakeholder resistance, timeline pressure
Team: Senior + 2-3 analysts + functional experts

Recommendation: Plan 12 months, invest heavily in
change management, secure executive sponsorship early."

Lessons Learned Extraction: AI automatically extracts lessons from each engagement:

  • Success factors identified from outcomes
  • Process learnings from consulting approach
  • Timeline/resource patterns
  • Industry-specific insights

RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation): Liqueo uses a knowledge retrieval + synthesis pattern: retrieve relevant engagements, then have AI synthesize patterns across them. This grounds synthesis in actual firm experience rather than generic knowledge.

9
How do they adapt existing knowledge for a new situation?

Liqueo supports adaptation through structured workflow and templated content:

Step 7: Create Output in the workflow enables this explicitly:

  • Start with content auto-populated from selected similar engagements
  • Edit and customize for the new situation's specific context
  • Add new engagement details (title, industry, value, duration)
  • Maintain structure (approach, outcomes, risks) while updating content

Template-Driven Adaptation: Rather than copy-paste, consultants work from templates extracted from similar cases:

  • Approach Template: Generic methodology from similar cases, customized for new client
  • Timeline Template: Standard phasing (assess → design → implement) with adjusted duration
  • Resource Template: Typical team structure, modified for scope
  • Risk Template: Common risks in similar engagements, evaluated for this client
Adaptation Workflow
Start: Find 3 similar "bank digital transformation" cases

Template from cases:
"Standard approach: Phase 1 (assess tech + org),
Phase 2 (design solution), Phase 3 (pilot + rollout).
Timeline: 12 months. Team: 1 partner, 2 managers, 3 analysts."

Adaptation for new client:
"Adjust Phase 2 timeline (this client has simpler systems),
reduce team to 1 manager + 2 analysts (smaller scope),
compress to 10-month timeline."

Result: Customized proposal in hours, not days

Content Reuse Without Copy-Paste: The workflow prevents mindless duplication. Consultants explicitly select which elements to reuse (Step 6: "Select Content"), then adapt them in Step 7. This is deliberate reuse, not copy-paste.

Metadata Tracking: When you create output from a workflow, Liqueo records which source engagements informed it. This traceability helps future users understand the logic behind your approach.

10
How do they contribute new knowledge back into the system?

Knowledge contribution happens at engagement completion, integrated into normal workflow:

Engagement Creation (Add Engagement Tab): When a consultant completes an engagement and wants to share learning:

  • Enter engagement details: title, industry, type, value, duration
  • Add consulting approach used
  • Document key outcomes achieved
  • Upload supporting files (proposals, reports, deliverables)
  • Optionally upload from OneDrive/SharePoint URLs

Content Extraction: System automatically extracts:

  • Text from uploaded documents (PDF, Word, Excel, CSV)
  • Metadata from engagement details
  • Structured information for indexing and search

Step 8-9: Tagging & Storage: In the structured workflow, engagement creation includes:

  • Tagging: Add descriptive tags (Cost Optimization, Digital Transformation, M&A, etc.)
  • Classification: Industry, transaction type, success metrics
  • Storage: Automatically indexed and made searchable
